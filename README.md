### Equipment Focus: Crate, Carriage/Carrier & Toys
These support safety, sleep, house training, and confidence.

**Crate (“Caging”)**  
- Choose a size that allows the puppy to stand, turn, and lie down comfortably. Use a divider for a small multipoo so it isn’t too large (helps potty training).  
- Make it positive only: treats, meals, favorite toys, soft bedding. Never for punishment.  
- Place near your bed for the first 2–3 weeks at night, then gradually move if desired.  
- Use for naps, overnight, and short alone-time practice. Cover partially for a den-like feel.  
- Expected: Puppies sleep 18–20 hours/day early on. Consistent crate time prevents overtired “zoomies” and accidents.

**Carriage / Carrier / Stroller**  
- Soft-sided carrier or small-breed stroller for outings before full vaccination and in Michigan winter.  
- Allows safe socialization (sights, sounds, people) without ground contact risk.  
- Practice short car rides early. Use for vet visits and errands.  
- Line with familiar bedding that has the puppy’s scent.

**Toys**  
- Rotate 4–6 toys to keep interest high.  
- Early (12–16 weeks): Soft plush, rope toys (supervised), frozen washcloths for teething, treat-dispensing toys.  
- Later: Durable chews, puzzle feeders, Kongs (stuffed and frozen), snuffle mats.  
- Avoid anything that can be shredded into small pieces. Supervise all chewing.  
- Replace worn toys promptly.

### Quick Reference Notes
- **Michigan specifics**: Rabies vaccination required for county licensing (typically by 4–6 months). Year-round parasite prevention is commonly recommended.
- **Spay**: For a small-breed female, many vets (including typical AAHA-aligned guidance) recommend before the first heat (~5–6 months) to maximize mammary tumor protection with low orthopedic risk. Final decision with Dr. Barkham.
- **Daily constants** (all year): Fresh water, consistent schedule, positive reinforcement, supervised interactions with senior dog (if present), and coat care.
